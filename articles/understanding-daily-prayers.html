Understanding Daily Prayers

The end-to-end meaning of every act and action in Salat — from intention to the final salam

Introduction

Salat (Namaz) is the second pillar of practice and the most frequently repeated act of worship in a believer's life — performed five times daily at Fajr, Zuhr, Asr, Maghrib, and Isha. It is described as the "pillar of religion" (Amud al-Din): if it is accepted, the rest of one's deeds are considered, and if it is rejected, the rest are not looked at.

Salat is not a sequence of movements to be rushed through. Each posture and phrase carries its own meaning, moving the believer through remembrance, humility, surrender, and gratitude — a complete conversation with Allah compressed into a few minutes, repeated throughout the day.

This guide walks through every act of prayer in order, with the Arabic, transliteration, and meaning of what is said at each stage, so that Salat can be offered with understanding (hudhoor-e-qalb — presence of the heart) rather than by memory alone.

The Order of Prayer, at a Glance

Each unit of prayer is called a Rak'ah. A two-Rak'ah prayer (like Fajr) repeats the cycle below twice; a four-Rak'ah prayer (like Zuhr, Asr, and Isha) repeats it with a short seated Tashahhud after the second Rak'ah as well as the last.

  1. Niyyah — Intention
  2. Qiyam & Takbiratul Ihram — Standing and the Opening Allahu Akbar
  3. Qira'at — Surah Al-Hamd, followed by another Surah (first two Rak'ahs only)
  4. Qunoot — a recommended supplication with raised hands (before the second Rak'ah's Ruku)
  5. Ruku — Bowing
  6. Qawmah — Rising from Ruku
  7. Sujud — Two Prostrations, with a brief sitting (Jalsa) between them
  8. Tashahhud — Testimony of Faith, after the second and final Rak'ah
  9. Salam — The Closing Greeting of Peace

1. Niyyah — Intention

1

Niyyah

نِيَّة

Prayer begins before the first word is spoken. Niyyah is the silent intention of the heart — not recited aloud — to perform this specific prayer (for example, the obligatory Zuhr prayer) purely and only for the sake of Allah's nearness (Qurbatan ilallah).

Meaning: every act of worship in Islam is judged by its intention. Niyyah turns a set of physical movements into an act of devotion, reminding the believer that they are standing before Allah by choice, not habit.

2. Qiyam & Takbiratul Ihram — Standing and the Opening Takbir

2

Qiyam

قِيَام

The believer stands upright, facing the Qiblah, feet together, hands resting at the sides (in the Shia method, unlike some other schools, the hands are not folded during Qiyam). This standing posture is itself an act of worship — a declaration of readiness before Allah.

Takbiratul Ihram

تَكْبِيرَةُ الْإِحْرَام

Raising both hands to the ears and declaring Allahu Akbar (Allah is the Greatest) formally opens the prayer. It is called Takbiratul Ihram — the Takbir of Sanctification — because from this moment, ordinary speech, eating, and unrelated movement become forbidden until the prayer is closed with Salam, just as certain acts become forbidden to a pilgrim in the state of Ihram.

اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ Allahu Akbar Allah is Greater — greater than every description, every comparison, and every worldly concern that might distract the heart.

It is recommended (mustahab) to recite Allahu Akbar seven times in total at the start of prayer, with brief supplications between repetitions, though reciting it once is sufficient to begin.

3. Qira'at — The Recitation

3

Surah Al-Hamd (Al-Fatiha)

سُورَةُ الْحَمْد

Every Rak'ah opens with Surah Al-Hamd, the opening chapter of the Qur'an. It is structured as a dialogue: the believer praises Allah, affirms that worship and help are sought from Him alone, and then asks to be guided on the straight path.

A Second Surah

سُورَة

In the first and second Rak'ah of every prayer, Surah Al-Hamd is followed by one complete additional Surah of the Qur'an (commonly Surah Al-Ikhlas or Surah Al-Qadr). In the third and fourth Rak'ahs, the believer may instead recite Surah Al-Hamd alone, or the Tasbihat-e-Arba'ah.

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ وَلَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ Subhanallahi wal-hamdu lillahi wa la ilaha illallahu wallahu akbar Glory be to Allah, all praise is for Allah, there is no god but Allah, and Allah is the Greatest — the Tasbihat-e-Arba'ah, recited quietly in the third and fourth Rak'ahs.

4. Qunoot — The Raised-Hand Supplication

4

Qunoot

قُنُوت

Before bowing into Ruku in the second Rak'ah of every prayer, it is strongly recommended to pause in Qiyam, raise both open hands to the level of the face (palms facing upward, as if asking), and supplicate in any language — in one's own words, or with a transmitted dua.

Meaning: Qunoot is the most personal moment of the obligatory prayer — a direct, unscripted plea placed in the middle of formal worship, a reminder that Salat is not only remembrance but also a channel for every need of the heart.

5. Ruku — Bowing

5

Ruku

رُكُوع

The believer bows forward until the palms rest flat on the knees and the back is held level, as if a vessel of water could sit upon it without spilling. In this posture — the body bent in submission — the following is recited, usually three times:

سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْعَظِيمِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ Subhana Rabbiyal 'Adheemi wa bihamdih Glory be to my Lord, the Magnificent, and praise be to Him.

Meaning: where Takbir declares Allah's greatness in words, Ruku declares it with the body — bowing the spine that is normally held upright for everything else, before the One Being deserving of such humility.

6. Qawmah — Rising from Ruku

6

Qawmah

قَوْمَة

Rising fully upright from Ruku, before moving to prostration, the believer pauses and says:

ArabicTransliterationEnglish translation
سَمِعَ اللَّهُ لِمَنْ حَمِدَهُ Sami'allahu liman hamidahAllah ne sun li us ki jis ne us ki hamd ki Allah hears the one who praises Him
اَللَّهُمَّ رَبَّنَا لَكَ الْحَمْدُ Allahumma Rabbana lakal hamdAey Allah, hamare Rab, saari tareef teray liye hai O Allah, our Lord, all praise belongs to You

Meaning: the moment of standing fully upright again — after humbling the body in Ruku — is itself treated as an independent pillar of prayer (a Rukn), a brief return to dignity before the deeper humility of Sujud.

7. Sujud — Prostration

7

Sujud

سُجُود

Sujud is the deepest point of physical humility in prayer: the forehead, both palms, both knees, and both big toes touch the ground together — seven points of the body lowered to the earth before Allah. It is performed twice in every Rak'ah, with a brief sitting between the two.

A distinguishing practice in Shia jurisprudence is that the forehead must rest on natural earth, stone, or an unprocessed plant material — commonly a small tablet of baked clay known as a Turbah (Mohr), often made from the soil of Karbala in remembrance of the sacrifice of Imam Husain (AS) — rather than on a carpet, cloth, or anything worn or eaten.

سُبْحَانَ رَبِّيَ الْأَعْلَى وَبِحَمْدِهِ Subhana Rabbiyal A'la wa bihamdih Glory be to my Lord, the Most High, and praise be to Him — recited (usually three times) in each of the two prostrations.

Meaning: if Ruku humbles the body, Sujud humbles it entirely — the highest part of the body, the forehead carrying the intellect and pride, is placed lowest, on the ground itself. It is described in Hadith as the point at which a servant is nearest to Allah.

Jalsa — Sitting Between the Two Prostrations

جَلْسَة

Between the first and second Sujud, the believer sits back briefly, resting on the left thigh, and says:

أَسْتَغْفِرُ اللَّهَ رَبِّي وَأَتُوبُ إِلَيْهِ Astaghfirullaha Rabbi wa atubu ilayh I seek the forgiveness of Allah, my Lord, and I turn to Him in repentance.

Meaning: even between two acts of worship, the believer pauses to seek forgiveness — a reminder that no deed, however devoted, replaces the ongoing need for Allah's mercy.

8. Tashahhud — The Testimony of Faith

8

Tashahhud

تَشَهُّد

After the second Rak'ah of every prayer (and again after the final Rak'ah), the believer sits and recites the Tashahhud — bearing witness to the oneness of Allah and the prophethood of Muhammad (SAWW), and sending blessings upon him and his purified family.

ArabicTransliterationEnglish translation
أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ Ashhadu an la ilaha illallahu wahdahu la shareeka lahMain gawahi deta hoon ke Allah ke siwa koi mabood nahin, woh akela hai, us ka koi shareek nahin I bear witness that there is no god except Allah, alone, without partner
وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ Wa ashhadu anna Muhammadan 'abduhu wa RasuluhAur main gawahi deta hoon ke Muhammad ﷺ us ke banday aur Rasool hain And I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger
اَللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِ مُحَمَّدٍ Allahumma salli 'ala Muhammadin wa Aali MuhammadAey Allah, Muhammad ﷺ aur Aal-e-Muhammad (ع) par apni rehmat nazil farma O Allah, send Your blessings upon Muhammad and the Family of Muhammad

Meaning: Tashahhud renews the two testimonies that define a Muslim's faith, and links every prayer back to the Prophet (SAWW) and his Ahlul Bayt (AS), through whom guidance was conveyed to humanity.

9. Salam — The Closing Greeting of Peace

9

Salam

سَلَام

After the Tashahhud of the final Rak'ah, the prayer is closed with the Salam — a greeting of peace that releases the believer from the state of prayer, much as Takbiratul Ihram had placed them into it.

ArabicTransliterationEnglish translation
اَلسَّلَامُ عَلَيْكَ أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ وَبَرَكَاتُهُ Assalamu 'alayka ayyuhan-Nabiyyu wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuhAey Nabi ﷺ, aap par salam ho, aur Allah ki rehmat aur barkatein Peace be upon you, O Prophet, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings
اَلسَّلَامُ عَلَيْنَا وَعَلَى عِبَادِ اللَّهِ الصَّالِحِينَ Assalamu 'alayna wa 'ala 'ibadillahis-saliheenHum par aur Allah ke neik bandon par salam ho Peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of Allah
اَلسَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ وَبَرَكَاتُهُ Assalamu 'alaykum wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuhAap sab par salam ho, aur Allah ki rehmat aur barkatein Peace be upon you all, and the mercy of Allah and His blessings

Meaning: the final Salam greets the Prophet (SAWW), every righteous servant of Allah past and present, and those present in prayer — closing an act of private devotion with a reminder of the wider community of believers it belongs to.

Meanings at a Glance

A short summary (Mukhtasar Tafsir-e-Mafhoom) of what each stage of prayer expresses.

Niyyah

Prayer is offered for Allah's nearness alone, not out of habit.

Namaz sirf Allah ki qurbat ke liye hai

Takbiratul Ihram

Allah is greater than everything that could distract the heart from Him.

Allah har us cheez se bara hai jo dil ko behka sake

Qira'at (Al-Hamd)

Praise, worship, and help belong to Allah alone; guidance is asked from Him.

Tareef, ibadat aur madad sirf Allah se hai

Qunoot

A personal, open plea placed in the middle of formal worship.

Namaz ke darmiyan dil ki khaalis dua

Ruku

The body bows in humility before the Magnificent.

Jism Allah ki azmat ke saamne jhukta hai

Qawmah

Standing upright again, every praise is attributed to Allah.

Dobara khade ho kar saari tareef Allah ko di jaati hai

Sujud

The forehead touches the earth — the nearest a servant comes to Allah.

Pesaani zameen par, banda Allah ke sab se qareeb

Tashahhud

Faith is renewed, linked to the Prophet ﷺ and his purified family (ع).

Imaan ka iqrar, Rasool ﷺ aur Aal (ع) se wabasta

Salam

Peace is sent to the Prophet ﷺ and to every righteous servant of Allah.

Salam Rasool ﷺ aur tamam neik bandon ke liye

Conclusion

From the silent Niyyah of the heart to the final Salam, every act of Salat carries a believer through praise, humility, supplication, and peace. It is a complete, repeated journey — Qiyam reminding the body it stands by choice, Ruku and Sujud reminding it that true dignity lies in humility before Allah, and the Tashahhud and Salam reminding the believer of the Prophet ﷺ, his family (ع), and the wider community of faith they belong to.

Offered with presence of heart (hudhoor-e-qalb), five times a day, Salat becomes less a duty to complete and more a conversation returned to — again and again, at its appointed times.

اللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِ مُحَمَّدٍ Allahumma salli 'ala Muhammadin wa Aali Muhammad O Allah, send Your blessings upon Muhammad and the Family of Muhammad.
