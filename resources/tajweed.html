Tajweed Rules
A Structured, Progressive Guide

Tajweed (تجويد) means "to make excellent." It is the set of rules for pronouncing the Qur'an exactly as it was revealed — where each letter is produced, how long each vowel is held, and how letters change when they meet. This guide breaks it into ten levels, each built on the one before.

10 levels
Self‑paced, level by level
Terms in Urdu (Zabar · Zair · Pesh · Jazm)
How to use this guide: work top to bottom — each level assumes you're comfortable with the last. For every rule, say the examples out loud slowly before moving on, and recite to a qualified teacher whenever you can. A page can show you the rule; only a trained ear can correct your pronunciation of it.

1

Articulation Points — Makharij al-Huruf

Where each letter is physically produced. Everything else in this guide depends on this.

Every Arabic letter is produced at a specific point in the mouth or throat. The 17 points group into five broad regions:

RegionLettersFeel for it
Throat (Al‑Halq)ء ہ · ع ح · غ خA tightening felt progressively lower in the throat — deepest for ء ہ, closest to the mouth for the last pair.
Tongue (Al‑Lisaan) — 10 sub‑pointsق ک ج ش ی ض ل ن ر ط د ت ص ز س ذ ث ظFrom the back of the tongue against the soft palate, forward to the tip against the gums and teeth.
Lips (Ash‑Shafatayn)ف ب م وLower lip to upper teeth for ف; both lips meeting for ب م و.
Nasal cavity (Al‑Khaishoom)GhunnahThe nasal hum carried on ن and م in certain positions — resonates through the nose, not the mouth.
Open mouth space (Al‑Jawf)ا و یThe long vowel (madd) letters — a pure vowel sound, air passing unobstructed.
Practice tip: drill one region per session. A common beginner mix‑up is ض vs ط vs د vs ت — all tongue‑tip letters but different points. Drill these four as a set, ideally against a teacher's or certified reciter's recording, since this is corrected by ear.
2

Letter Characteristics — Sifaat

Where a letter comes from (Level 1) tells you its address; Sifaat tell you its texture.

PairMeaningExample contrast
Hams / JahrWhispered (breath escapes) vs voiced (vocal cords engaged)س is Hams; ز is Jahr
Shiddah / RakhawahSound stops abruptly vs flows and continuesب stops sharply; ف can be drawn out
Isteelaa / IstifaalTongue raised → heavy sound, vs tongue flat → light soundط is heavy; ت is its light near‑twin
Idhlaaq / IsmaatProduced easily at the tongue‑tip/lips vs needs more effortر flows easily; ض takes more tongue effort

The seven heavy letters (Huroof al‑Tafkheem) are always pronounced with a thickened, rounded mouth shape:

ص ض ط ظ ق خ غ — everything else is naturally light (Tarqeeq) unless a later rule (Level 9, Ra) makes it heavy in context.
Practice tip: say ط and ت back‑to‑back — same tongue point, but ط should sound fuller and rounder. If you can't hear the difference yet, that's normal; it sharpens with repetition.
3

Noon Saakin & Tanween Rules

What happens when a silent noon (ن) or tanween (ً ٍ ٌ) meets the next letter — the most‑tested area in Tajweed. Colours follow the key above.

RuleTrigger lettersWhat happensExample
Izhar
clear — uncoloured		 ء ہ ع ح غ خ Pronounced fully and clearly — no change مِن عِلْمٍ
Idghaam
merging		 ی ر م ل و ن Fully merges into the next letter (with Ghunnah for ينمو; without for ر ل) مَن یَّقُل
Iqlab (Qalb)
conversion		 ب Converts to a hidden م sound, with Ghunnah, before pronouncing ب مِن بَعْدِ
Ikhfa
hiding		 the remaining 15 letters Pronounced lightly, nasalized — neither fully clear nor fully merged مِنكُم
Practice order: Iqlab/Qalb first (only one trigger letter, ب — easiest to spot), then Izhar (6 clear throat letters), then Idghaam, then Ikhfa last, since it's the most subtle of the four. Only combine all four once each is solid alone.
Exception: Idghaam only applies across a word boundary. When Noon Saakin and an Idghaam letter fall inside the same word, it stays Izhar instead — this happens in exactly four words in the whole Qur'an: دُنْیَا، صِنْوَان، قِنْوَان، بُنْیَان.
4

Meem Saakin Rules

The same idea as Level 3, for a silent meem (م) — three rules instead of four, all about the lips. Colours follow the key above.

RuleTriggerWhat happensExample
Ikhfa Meem Saakin ب Lips stay closed; meem is hidden with a light nasal sound before releasing into ب تَرمِیہِم بِحِجارَةٍ
Idghaam Meem Saakin م The two meems merge into one, held with Ghunnah لَہُم مَّا
Izhar Shafawi
clear — uncoloured		 remaining 26 letters Pronounced normally and clearly, lips closing and releasing crisply أَنعَمتَ
Common mistake: letting Izhar Shafawi blend into the next letter when it's another lip letter (و, ب) — keep the lips closing fully each time.

6

Ghunnah & Shaddah Rules

Ghunnah — the nasal hum of ن and م — isolated as its own skill, at full strength. Colours follow the key above.

The core rule: whenever م or ن carries a shaddah (ّ), it must be held with a full nasal hum for about 2 counts (roughly the time it takes to say "1‑2" at a natural pace). This is Ghunnah Musaddadah — the strongest, clearest ghunnah in all of Tajweed, stronger than the ghunnah inside Ikhfa or Idghaam.

إِنَّhold the hum on the doubled ن for ~2 counts before releasing the Zabar
ثُمَّhold the hum on the doubled م for ~2 counts
Common mistake: rushing a shaddah meem/noon as if it were a single, instant letter. The shaddah means the letter is held in time — skipping that changes the whole word's rhythm, and it's one of the more noticeable errors to a trained ear.
7

Qalqalah

The "echoing" letters — a short, controlled bounce on five letters whenever they carry no vowel. Colours follow the key above.

ق ط ب ج د mnemonic: qutbu jad
PositionStrengthExample
Sughra (minor) — saakin mid‑word, not at a stopLighter bounceیَقْطَعُونَ
Kubra (major) — saakin because you stopped (waqf) on itStronger, more pronounced bounceوَالفَلَقْ
The sound is a small, controlled bounce of air at the letter's own point of articulation — never a hard extra vowel (don't say "qa‑TA" with a full vowel). Just a crisp separation that makes the letter audible even with no vowel of its own.
8

Madd — Elongation Rules

How long to hold a vowel, measured in counts. The most layered topic in this guide.

Natural Madd (Madd Asli / Tabee'i) — always 2 counts. The three madd letters ا و ی, when not followed by a hamza or a saakin letter, are simply held for 2 counts: قَالَ.

Secondary Madds (Madd Far'i) — length depends on the cause:

TypeCauseLengthExample
Muttasil
connected		madd letter + hamza, same word4–5 countsجاء
Munfasil
separated		madd letter ends one word, hamza starts the next4–5 counts
(often 2 for some reciters)		یا أَیُّہَا
Laazim
obligatory		madd letter + a permanently saakin letter6 counts, alwaysالصَّاخَّة
'Aarid lis‑Sukoon
due to stopping		madd letter + a letter saakin only because you stopped there2, 4, or 6
(reciter's choice, kept consistent)		العَالَمِین
Practice order: master the 2‑count natural madd until it's automatic, then Madd Laazim (fixed 6 counts, easy to spot — often marked with an elongated bar in some prints), before tackling Muttasil and Munfasil, which need you to notice the hamza across or within word boundaries.
9

Rules of Ra

Most letters are consistently heavy or light (Level 2) — ر changes with context.

Heavy · Tafkheem when:

  • It has Zabar or Pesh: رَبَّنَا
  • It is saakin (Jazm) and preceded by a Zabar or Pesh: یَرْجِعُون
  • It is saakin (Jazm) and preceded by a Zair, but followed by a heavy letter: قِرْطَاس

Light · Tarqeeq when:

  • It has Zair: رِجْس
  • It is saakin (Jazm) and preceded by a Zair, with no heavy letter following: فِرْعَوْن
Either is accepted when ر is saakin (Jazm) at the end of a word you're stopping on, preceded by a saakin letter that itself follows a Zair — reciters genuinely differ here.
10

Waqf — Stopping Signs

The marks printed in the Qur'an that tell you where, and how, to pause.

SymbolNameMeaning
مWaqf LazimMust stop here — continuing changes the meaning
لاWaqf Mamnoo'Do not stop here — continue to the next word
جWaqf JaizYou may stop or continue — either is fine
قلیPreferable to stopStopping is slightly better than continuing
صلیPreferable to continueContinuing is slightly better than stopping
قفQifA reminder to pause here
∴ ∴Mu'aanaqahPaired stops — stop at one of the two, never both
Whenever you stop — at a sign, or just to breathe — the last letter becomes saakin (Jazm), which can trigger Qalqalah Kubra (Level 7) or Madd 'Aarid lis‑Sukoon (Level 8). That's why those two rules are easier once you understand how stopping works.

Practice Sequence

Work through the levels in order — each one builds on the last. Move on only once a level feels steady; this is a guide, not a deadline.

  • 1Articulation Points— drill letter by letter until each feels distinct
  • 2Sifaat— add texture and weight to letters you already know
  • 3Noon Saakin & Tanween— the biggest rule set; one sub‑rule at a time
  • 4Meem Saakin— faster, mirrors Level 3's logic
  • 5Laam Saakin— mostly a reading‑recognition skill
  • 6Ghunnah & Shaddah— focus on timing and duration
  • 7Qalqalah— short, focused drill on 5 letters
  • 8Madd— the most layered; take it slower than the rest
  • 9Rules of Ra— builds on Levels 2 and 8
  • 10Waqf— ties everything together
Self‑check milestone Can you recite Al‑Fatiha (or another short, memorized surah) applying every rule learned so far, without consciously calculating each one? If yes, move on. If you're still working it out mid‑recitation, stay on that level a little longer.
A note on sources: this guide covers the mainstream Hafs 'an Asim rules followed by the great majority of printed Qur'ans and reciters worldwide. A few details (count‑lengths for Madd Munfasil, for example) vary slightly between recognized schools of recitation — that's normal, not a contradiction.
Next step: find a certified Tajweed teacher or a local Qur'an circle, in person or online, to recite to once you've worked through a level here. Self‑study builds understanding of the rules; a teacher's ear is what actually corrects your pronunciation.

Glossary

Every term used in this guide, in English and Romanized Urdu, grouped by where it appears.

Core Concepts

Tajweedتجوید

Literally "to make excellent" — the full set of rules for reciting the Qur'an correctly.

Makhraj (pl. Makharij)مخرج

The exact point in the mouth or throat where a letter is produced (Level 1).

Sifaatصفات

The "characteristics" or texture of a letter — heavy/light, whispered/voiced, and so on (Level 2).

Tafkheemheavy

A thickened, rounded pronunciation given to certain letters, always or in context.

Tarqeeqlight

The thin, plain pronunciation — the default for most letters.

Vowels & Signs — Urdu Names

ZabarArabic: Fathaَ

The short "a" vowel sign, written as a small diagonal stroke above a letter.

ZairArabic: Kasraِ

The short "i" vowel sign, written as a small diagonal stroke below a letter.

PeshArabic: Dammaُ

The short "u" vowel sign, written as a small loop above a letter.

JazmArabic: Saakinْ

The sign showing a letter carries no vowel at all — a small circle above it.

Tashdeedletter: Mushaddadّ

The doubling sign (shaddah) — the letter is pronounced twice, held, not rushed.

Tanween

Double vowel signs at a word's end (ً ٍ ٌ) that add an "n" sound — treated like Noon Saakin for the rules in Level 3.

Noon Saakin & Tanween Rules (Level 3)

Izharclearاظہار

The Noon Saakin is pronounced fully and clearly, with no change.

Idghaammergingادغام

The Noon Saakin fully merges into the next letter.

Idghaam Ma'al Ghunnah

Idghaam that keeps the nasal hum — with ي ن م و.

Idghaam Bila Ghunnah

Idghaam with no nasal hum — with ر ل only.

Iqlab= Qalbاقلاب

The Noon Saakin converts to a hidden Meem sound before ب, with Ghunnah.

Ikhfahidingاخفاء

The Noon Saakin is pronounced lightly and nasalized — neither clear nor merged.

Meem Saakin Rules (Level 4)

Ikhfa Shafawi= Ikhfa Meem Saakin

Meem Saakin hidden with a light nasal sound before ب.

Idghaam Shafawi= Idghaam Meem Saakin

Two meems merging into one, held with Ghunnah.

Izhar Shafawiclear

Meem Saakin pronounced normally before any of the other 26 letters.

Shafawi

"Labial" — of the lips. Used because Meem itself is a lip letter.

Laam, Ghunnah & Qalqalah (Levels 5–7)

Laam Shamsiyyah"sun laam"

The ل of ال is silent and merges into a following sun letter, which doubles.

Laam Qamariyyah"moon laam"

The ل of ال is pronounced clearly before a moon letter.

Ghunnahغنہ

The nasal hum carried on ن and م, strongest when either carries a Tashdeed.

Ghunnah Musaddadah

The full, 2‑count nasal hum on a Mushaddad (doubled) Noon or Meem — the strongest Ghunnah.

Qalqalahechoingقلقلہ

A short bounce of sound on ق ط ب ج د when any of them carries Jazm.

Qalqalah Sughraminor

Qalqalah on a letter that is Jazm in the middle of a word.

Qalqalah Kubramajor

Qalqalah on a letter that is Jazm only because you stopped (Waqf) on it — stronger bounce.

Madd & Ra (Levels 8–9)

Maddelongationمد

Holding a vowel sound for a set number of counts.

Madd Asli= Madd Tabee'i

The natural, always‑2‑count madd on ا و ی with no hamza or Jazm letter following.

Madd Far'i

Any "secondary" madd whose length is extended by a cause — hamza, a permanent Jazm letter, or a stop.

Madd Muttasilconnected

Madd letter and hamza in the same word — 4–5 counts.

Madd Munfasilseparated

Madd letter ends one word, hamza starts the next — 4–5 counts (often shortened).

Madd Laazimobligatory

Madd letter followed by a permanently Jazm letter — always 6 counts.

Madd 'Aarid lis‑Sukoondue to stopping

Madd letter followed by a letter that is Jazm only because you stopped — 2, 4, or 6 counts, reciter's choice.

Waqf — Stopping Signs (Level 10)

Waqfstoppingوقف

Pausing in recitation — governed by signs printed in the Qur'an.

Waqf Lazimم

An obligatory stop — continuing would change the meaning.

Waqf Mamnoo'لا

A prohibited stop — you must continue to the next word.

Waqf Jaizج

A permissible stop — stopping or continuing are both fine.

Qifقف

A reminder sign to pause.

Mu'aanaqahpaired stops

Two sets of three dots — stop at one set only, never both.
