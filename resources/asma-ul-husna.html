The 99 Beautiful Names of Allah

Asma ul Husna (أسماء الله الحسنى) — the Most Beautiful Names — are the attributes by which Allah has described Himself in the Qur'an and through the Ahlul Bayt (AS). Each one is a doorway: to know the Name is to begin to reflect it.

"And to Allah belong the Most Beautiful Names, so call upon Him by them." Surah Al-A'raf, 7:180
99 names Calligraphy · Romanized Urdu · English · Meaning Flashcards to help you memorize

Why memorize the Names?

The first human being, Adam (AS), was "taught all the Names" (2:31) — Allah made him conscious of the attributes of divinity already placed within him. Learning Asma ul Husna is a continuation of that same teaching: each Name is both a description of Allah and an invitation to the believer.

Knowledge

To call on Allah correctly, you first have to know Him by what He has named Himself — not by guesswork or habit. Memorizing the Names is how that knowledge becomes available the moment you need it, in dua or in difficulty.

Process

It isn't enough to recite a Name or know its dictionary meaning. Each one asks to be reflected on — Al-Hayy when you think about life, As-Sabur when you're tested — until the meaning settles somewhere deeper than memory.

Action

The Names are meant to be lived, not just recited. Ar-Rahim asks for mercy toward others, Al-'Adl asks for justice, Al-Karim asks for generosity — knowing the Name is the start of carrying its quality into how you treat people.

"Allah has ninety-nine Names — one hundred less one. Whoever preserves and takes account of them will enter Paradise." A widely reported hadith on the Names of Allah
